## v12.86

Phase 1b continued — **`household_signals.py`**, which proposes household groupings from a spreadsheet and merges nothing.

Implements plan §7.3: use all four signals, because no single one is sufficient and each finds groupings the others miss — surname+road clustering, household/"paid by" notes, £0 fee, and shared email address, unioned so they combine rather than compete. Every output is a PROPOSAL carrying its evidence and a count of how many independent signals agree. A count, not a percentage: a made-up 87% invites people to accept anything above 80, whereas "three signals agree, here they are" is something a treasurer can actually reason about.

Nothing is ever merged automatically. Deciding that two people at one address share a membership is deciding who owes money and who receives the renewal letter, and an address is not evidence of a household — postcodes cover many addresses, flats share a street, and two people at one address may hold two memberships legitimately.

**Blind spots are output, not silence.** Members the signals cannot reach are reported: notes naming somebody the sheet does not contain, £0-fee members no other signal grouped, and members with no road name who are invisible to clustering. The plan puts the treasurer's review at about half an hour, which only holds if she is told where the gaps are rather than finding them next renewal.

Four faults found by running against the live register rather than by unit tests:

* **A struck-through, deceased member was suggested as a household's primary contact.** Run without flags on the RA sheet, the Robinson household proposed Margaret Robinson — a row that is struck through and annotated "signed up twice" — as the person who receives the renewal letter. The household also contains Keith, struck through and annotated "Deceased". That is precisely the failure v12.83 and v12.84 were spent preventing, arriving through the import instead of through the UI. `propose_households()` now takes `flagged_rows` (from sheetscan's strikethrough detection plus deceased/duplicate notes): flagged members still JOIN their household, because the payment history is theirs and the group owns it, but are never suggested as primary. With flags applied the same household now proposes Rita.
* **"Paid by Bank Transfer" was read as a member called Bank Transfer.** The notes column doubles as a payment-method note, so four blind spots were fictional — including Steve Wherlock, who was correctly grouped by two other signals and reported as unreachable anyway. A note now only implies a household if it says so in words; "paid by cheque" is not a household signal.
* **The name capture swallowed the rest of the note.** The character class has to allow spaces and hyphens, so "Household membership with LAURA CHILDS - paid by Laura" captured all nine words and matched nobody — and Laura Childs, who is in the sheet, came back as a blind spot. Captures now stop at a separator or a continuation word. This recovered a real cross-surname household that only the note could find, which is exactly what §7.3 says notes are for.
* **A note on a member's own row was reported as unresolved.** "Family Membership - paid by Sue", written on Sue's row, means Sue paid.

Also: names are unicode-folded before comparison, because the register contains both `O'Brien` (U+0027) and `O’Brien` (U+2019) — one family to a human, two dictionary keys to a parser, and a household that silently fails to cluster. Road names fold their suffixes, so "Doris Road" and "doris rd" are one key. A first name alone resolves only when it is unique in the sheet or unique among people on that member's road; two Sams on different roads is refused and surfaced rather than guessed.

Verified: 12 unit checks plus a full run against the live 9 September register — 300 members, **27 proposals reaching 57 members**, 243 single-member households, and 4 remaining blind spots, every one genuinely needing a person: a payer absent from the sheet, a different-surname payer, and two spelling variances (McCluskey/McLuskey, Marion/Marian). The plan's April estimate was 22 clusters over 60 members, so the shape holds. household_signals.py, config.py.

STILL TO COME in Phase 1b: the wizard UI, advance-payment splitting on the import path, and the review queue these feed.

## v12.85

Phase 1b, first half — the three things the import wizard has to get right before there is any point building a wizard. No behaviour changes for anyone not running an import.

NEW — **`events.py`: the import suppression boundary, built before the thing it guards.** Plan §8.1 is the highest-severity risk in the project: importing ~300 RA members with their historic payment dates, once Phase 2 adds `payment.recorded`, would advance every renewal date and queue hundreds of emails to real people about money they paid in 2025. The fix is not to remember to suppress events when Phase 2 lands — it is for the event system to be born suppressed inside an import. `fire_event()` dispatches to a handler registry that is empty until Phase 2, so today this changes nothing, which is the point: the boundary exists and is tested before there is anything to leak. Suppression is thread-local (a long import must not silence a concurrent request — gunicorn runs threads and a global flag would do exactly that), a context manager rather than a flag (an import that raises at row 57 cannot leave events off for the rest of the process), and counted rather than silent (an import reporting "312 events suppressed" is auditable; one reporting nothing is indistinguishable from one where suppression failed). The three writing import routes carry `@suppressed_import(...)` on the route itself rather than around the row loop, so the boundary covers the early returns and error paths too. The count now appears in the import response and the audit entry.

NEW — **`scripts/check_import_suppression.py`.** A unit test proves the boundary works; it cannot prove nobody has since added a fourth import route without it. This walks the AST, finds every POST route whose path contains `/import/` and is not an analyse/preview step, and fails if one lacks the decorator. Verified both ways: clean on the tree, exit 1 on a planted unguarded route.

NEW — **`dateparse.py`: reads dates without guessing.** The RA's 26/27 column holds 52 text strings against 14 real dates in three shapes — `21/3/26`, `20/4/2027`, `29/08/26` — plus `00/00/00` as a sentinel. `3/4/26` is 3 April to a British treasurer and 4 March to a parser written in the wrong hemisphere, and nothing about the stored value says which happened. So the convention is inferred ONCE per column from the values that prove it (a component above 12 can only be a day), and where the evidence is absent or contradictory every ambiguous value is **flagged rather than defaulted**. Sentinels are recognised as conventions, not failures. Two-digit years resolve against a window anchored on today.

NEW — **`sheetscan.py`: reads what the spreadsheet encodes in formatting.** Plan §8.2, the risk specific to this migration. In the RA register 105 rows are shaded grey, 6 cells in the 26/27 column are filled red, 4 rows are struck through, and one struck row reads "Deceased" while carrying a recorded payment — on values alone that imports as an active, paid member who gets a renewal reminder next year. The scanner reports fill clusters, strikethrough, font colours, per-column type mixes and distinct values. It deliberately does NOT decide what grey means: the same colour means "removed from the mailing list" in one organisation and "left" in another, and I got the RA's own convention backwards on the first pass by inferring it from correlation. A person declares the meaning in the wizard, as configuration, so the next organisation's yellow costs nobody any code.

Two faults the real sheet found that invented test data would not have:

* **`two_digit_year` was an issue; it is now a note.** On the RA's 26/27 column that put 16 rows in front of a human who had nothing to decide about any of them. A review queue that cries wolf sixteen times is one nobody reads the seventeenth time. Issues are things a person must decide; notes are recorded and do not block.
* **Repeated real dates were reported as mysterious conventions — 74 of them.** The convention check was running against `str(cell.value)`, which for a cell Excel had already typed is `'2025-09-27 00:00:00'` — a string no date parser accepts. It now runs only against values that were TYPED as text. Relatedly, a fill or font colour covering ≥90% of rows is now classified as the sheet's house style and excluded: the RA register sets every row's text blue, and reporting that as a 302-row signal would have buried the 4 rows that matter.

Verified: 23 checks plus a full run against the live 9 September RA register. The parser was pointed at all 424 date values across three columns and produced a review queue of **five rows** — Neall's `00/00/00` sentinel in two columns, and the three future-dated payments (Brien, Mason, Bell). Those are exactly the five found by hand over two sessions, with no false positives, and two of them were missed on the first manual pass because they were stored as text. The scanner independently found all 105 grey rows, all 4 struck rows, the 6 red cells, and correctly identified the Renewal Date column by role. It also reports that `ALL MEMBERS x1` carries no formatting markings at all, which corroborates that sheet being a derived copy rather than the live register.

## v12.84

D3 passed its re-test — all five checks, including the three that must be ALLOWED. The guard is specific rather than blunt, and the MAJOR from the v2 report is closed. Following the one open question that run left behind found a bigger hole of exactly the same shape.

MAJOR — **deceased members were reachable by mailshot.** v12.78 seeded the `is_deceased` flag under this comment: *"the is_deceased flag set below is what drives the comms exclusion and the reassignment guard."* It drove the reassignment guard. There was no comms exclusion anywhere — outside `db.py`, `is_deceased` was read only by `groups.status_is_deceased()`. Deceased members were dropped from a mailshot purely as a side effect of their behaviour being `leaver`, which means status filter "Active" excluded them by accident while **"Leaver" and "all" both included them**. A win-back mailshot to lapsed members — an obvious thing for a residents association to send — would have reached the dead. Same failure the D3 guard exists to prevent, through a different door, and the same bug class as D3 itself: a claim in a comment with no code behind it.

`_get_recipients()` now excludes any member whose status carries `is_deceased`, unconditionally, before any filter is applied. This is deliberately a HARD exclusion and not another filter value: every other status is an administrative state somebody might legitimately want to mail, this one is not, and the cost of getting it wrong is a letter to a bereaved household. No override parameter was added — an escape hatch on this guard is the thing that would eventually get used. It keys off the FLAG, not the name "Deceased", so an organisation that renames the status or flags one of its own is covered. `_get_recipients` is the only path to the member mailer, so this is the single chokepoint.

BUG — **the send endpoint dropped every filter but the session.** `api_mailshots_send` passed `status_filter=None` under the comment *"defaults to active behaviour"* — it does not, it applies no status restriction at all — and silently discarded `member_type_filter` and `flag_rule_ids`, so a send through that branch could reach a wider audience than the preview that produced it. The UI always posts an explicit recipient list and takes the other branch, so it was never hit in normal use, but it is a live `mailshots.send` path. It now honours what it was asked for. The `_get_recipients` docstring made the same false "default: Active only" claim and has been corrected rather than quietly deleted.

UI — **a household whose primary contact has died now says so.** The D3e run ended with the Households page reading "Household · 1 member / Bob Smith ★" with no indication whatever that Bob was deceased, and no mechanism that would ever change it: the status guard correctly allows the change when there is nobody to hand over to, and `archive_or_delete_group()` fires only when a member is REMOVED from a group, so dying leaves the record sitting there indefinitely. Group rows now carry `primary_deceased` and per-member `is_deceased`; the card shows a red "Primary contact deceased" badge first in the badge list, prompts "choose a new primary contact", and renders that member's chip struck back. The mailshot recipient box now states plainly that deceased members are never included, so the rule is visible rather than mysterious.

Verified: 17 checks. Nine on the recipient query against the real UNION shape — the gap reproduced first (Leaver and all both reaching the deceased without the guard), then each filter value, both halves of the UNION, a renamed status, and an organisation's own custom status flagged `is_deceased`. Seven on the badge logic including the D3e sole-member case, a non-primary death (no badge — nothing to action), and a group with no primary set. ## v12.82

Two config-integrity fixes behind a question the test pass raised, plus one display decision from Nik.

BUG — **the app minted indistinguishable duplicate fields on request.** The tester found "Sent Welcome" listed twice in the alert rule builder, as `sent_welcome` and `sent_welcome_1`. Neither is seeded anywhere in code: both were created by hand through the Custom Fields page, and `POST /api/admin/field-definitions` accepted the second one, silently uniquified the key and returned 201. Two fields carrying the same label are indistinguishable in every picker in the app, so whoever records the data has no way to know which of the pair they are filling in, and the answers split across both. The label collision IS the error, so it now returns 409 naming the existing field, and `PUT` refuses a rename onto another field's label for the same reason. The key uniquifier stays as a backstop for distinct labels that slugify the same ("E-mail" / "E mail").

BUG — **deleting a custom field could erase recorded member data with no warning.** The delete guard counted only `member_type_fields`, i.e. whether the field was still ASSIGNED to a member type. But `member_field_values.field_id` is `ON DELETE CASCADE` and `get_db()` sets `PRAGMA foreign_keys = ON`, so the DELETE takes every recorded value with it. A field detached from its type but still holding a year of answers — exactly the state of a duplicate someone is about to tidy up — passed the guard and deleted silently. It now counts non-blank values too and refuses with 409 stating how many members would lose data; `?force=1` overrides deliberately, and the audit entry records the count erased and whether it was forced. The field list and the field builder now report `values_recorded` alongside `assigned_to`, the Delete button is hidden while a field holds values, and where a duplicate label already exists the picker shows each one's key and value count so the pair can be told apart.

CHANGE (Nik's decision) — voided payments stay visible in the member payment history, greyed, without strikethrough. They were already shown; the row carried both 45% opacity and a line-through, which made the amount hard to read for no gain, since the VOID badge already says what happened.

Verified: 5 checks against the real schema — `values_recorded` ignoring NULL and whitespace-only values, the create guard matching across case and surrounding whitespace while leaving a near-miss label alone, the rename guard excluding the field's own row, the precise hole (`assigned_to = 0` with values recorded, which the old guard passed and the new one refuses), and that the cascade really does destroy the values with foreign keys on. blueprints/admin.py, templates/admin/field_builder.html, templates/members.html, config.py.

## v12.80

BUG (spotted by Nik) — the current membership period's start and end dates were optional, and one feature was silently WRONG without them rather than merely unavailable.

`_sessions_remaining_map` bounds its attendance count by the period's dates. With the dates blank the date filter was simply omitted, so EVERY session the member had ever attended counted against the block of sessions they had just bought. A member with three years of history who bought a block of 10 would show as nearly out of sessions, and a rule such as "paid sessions remaining is under 2" would flag exactly the people who had just paid. Nothing errored; the numbers were just quietly wrong. It now refuses to compute without both bounds and logs why — a member with no value for the field matches no numeric condition, which is the safe direction. It logs with print() rather than current_app.logger, because this runs under the nightly scheduler where there is no app context.

The dates are consequently REQUIRED, not optional: they define what counts as inside the period, which is also what period-aligned renewal anchoring resolves against. `POST /api/payments/current-period` rejects a missing start or end with a message saying why; the admin form marks both fields required, states the reason, and validates before submitting.

Also fixed in the v12.78 migration: it would happily create a `membership_periods` row with empty start and end dates if the old settings had none — a period that cannot bound anything. It now skips such a period, logs a warning naming it, and deliberately does NOT set its completion marker, so the migration retries on the next restart and self-heals the moment an admin fills the dates in.

Verified: 122 checks — the 110 from v12.79 unchanged, plus 12 new (sessions_remaining returning nothing rather than a wrong number when dates are missing, and the correct 8 once they are set, with pre-period attendance proven not to count; the four API rejection paths; the migration refusing to create a dateless period, leaving its marker unset, and then migrating correctly once the dates exist). blueprints/alerts.py, blueprints/payments.py, db.py, templates/admin/payments.html, config.py.

## v12.78

FEATURE (Automations & Member Groups plan, Phase 1a) — members can be grouped into households that are billed together. One payment against a group covers every member of it, and a renewal reminder goes to the group's primary contact rather than to everybody in it. Driven by the Residents Association, whose subscription is per household, but the same shape as siblings on one family subscription at the club — so this is core, not a per-client feature, and it is inert until switched on.

New tables: `group_types` (name, slug, is_billing_unit, icon, colour, active, sort_order) with a PARTIAL UNIQUE INDEX so the database itself permits only one billing type per organisation; `member_groups` (name, group_type_id, primary_member_id, notes, is_active/archived_at/archived_by); `member_group_members` junction; `membership_periods` (name, start/end, is_current). New columns: `member_payments.group_id` (NULL = an individual payment, which is every existing row); `member_statuses.is_deceased`; `member_types.billing_exempt`.

New top-level `groups.py` holds every resolution rule in one place, and `blueprints/groups.py` exposes it. Resolution ALWAYS goes through the junction — there is deliberately no group id echoed onto the members row, that being the echo-column bug class the July audits kept finding.

THE INVARIANTS, and why each is a hard block rather than a warning: (1) A member belongs to at most ONE billing group. A second is refused, never silently swapped — which group owes the money is not a question the system should answer on someone's behalf. The rule spans the junction and group_types so it cannot be a table constraint; it is enforced in the API and REPAIRED on every boot by a self-healing reconcile, keeping the most recent membership, exactly as member_sessions does. (2) A billing group always has a primary contact. Removing the primary, or marking them deceased, is blocked until another member is nominated — this is the guard that stops next year's renewal reminder being addressed to somebody who has died, and it is wired into the member status-change endpoint, not merely available. (3) The primary contact is only ever auto-SET when a group has none; it is never auto-reassigned because a different person paid. (4) A group whose last member leaves is ARCHIVED if it has any payment history and DELETED only if it has none — the group owns that history and the treasurer must still be able to answer what an address paid three years ago. An archived group keeps its payments, stops covering members, and disappears from the default listing.

Also: `Deceased` seeded as a member status with behaviour `leaver`, so every existing active-member query excludes it unchanged; the is_deceased FLAG rather than the name drives the guard, so an organisation can rename it. `member_types.billing_exempt` marks honorary members and staff, who need no billing group and must never be chased by the unpaid rule. `membership_periods` replaces the free-text period plus the current_membership_period settings — the setting is migrated into the first row on boot and kept in step when the current period changes, so existing readers keep working. New `groups.view` / `groups.manage` permissions added to BOTH ALL_PERMISSIONS and DEFAULT_ROLE_PERMISSIONS. New admin page at /admin/groups with a Settings card.

ADVANCE PAYMENTS: a payment may now cover several membership periods at once — the Residents Association's "£10 to cover 2025 & 2026" case, which their spreadsheet records in a notes field. `POST /api/members/<id>/payments` accepts `periods: [...]` alongside the existing single `period`, and writes ONE ROW PER PERIOD sharing a new `member_payments.advance_ref` token. Recording £10 as a single row against one period would leave the second year reading as unpaid and the renewal date wrong; two rows make both years true. The total is split across the rows with any remainder on the first, and a NULL amount stays NULL rather than becoming zeros, which would misreport the year's takings. Voiding ANY row of an advance payment voids all of them and reports how many — voiding only the row that was clicked would leave the member paid for a year they never paid for. The same endpoint gained `paid_for: 'member' | 'group'`, which is what finally WRITES `member_payments.group_id`: a group payment covers every member of the group, and is rejected with a clear message if the member is not in one. Default stays 'member', single-period payments are byte-for-byte unchanged, and `advance_ref` is NULL on every existing row. Note the advance_ref index is created after the ALTER rather than in the CREATE TABLE block — on a fresh install the column does not exist while that block runs.
